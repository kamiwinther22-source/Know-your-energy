# Know Your Energy: handoff (short on purpose)

The owner does not code. Do not explain how you did things. Say what happened and what you need from them.

## WORKING RULES
1. **"Do it", "make it live", "push it", "go" or "yes I want that" means: do it and put it live NOW.** Merge to `main`, check the live site, send a picture. Never ask again. This is the most important rule. If they have not said go, show before live.
2. **Answer the owner's message first**, in a few words, before doing anything else. They watch the thread and interrupt when they cannot tell what is happening.
3. **Do what is said, and no more.** If something is obviously the same problem as the one just fixed, fix it too and say so in a line. Do not widen a rule the owner stated about one thing to the whole site, unless they say it applies everywhere.
4. **Clear problems in what you just made: fix them and show them.** The owner expects initiative ("why haven't you changed it?", "be proactive"). When they are only venting about the overall look, do not build; wait for a direction.
5. **If the owner asks whether something is right and it is not, make it right, show it, and wait for "go".**
6. **Show the whole screen or page** for any layout change, not crops. For the form, show Relational mode (Person 1 and Person 2). For a one-word edit, a picture of that spot is enough.
7. **Phone first.** Desktop and tablet are for a later thread. Phone-only code is the last block of `red-look.css` plus small scripts in `index.html`.
8. **Small tasks stay small.** "Move the button up" is a small change. Do not rebuild layouts.
9. **Short messages.** No "how". The owner will not read it.
10. **Test proportionately.** A handful of phone sizes is normal (320x568, 375x629, 390x664, 412x770, 430x739). Test more only when a fit could fail between them.

## DESIGN STANCE: show the range, not the average
The owner is paying for taste and expertise, and said so: "do not give me what the average person wants", "the safe answer is the wrong answer", "I want the full range, with pros and cons".
- **Open wide first.** For any look decision, think of the first twenty ideas, then deliberately go past them. Palettes, materials, type, layout and motion should come from the whole field of design (editorial, product, architecture, jewelry, packaging, film, print, instruments), not from the astrology-site template.
- **Show 2 to 4 options that are truly different from each other**, each large, in place on the real page, with one line each on why it could work, what it costs, and the risk. Not several near-identical variations. Say which one you would choose and why.
- **Rejections are information, not fences.** "I hate the grey base" tells you what the owner reacts to. It does not ban grey, pearl, black, white, blue or satin as ideas. The owner said: "I rejected the way you were doing it. There are 100 other ways." Find the other ways. Read the reason behind every rejection and design for the reason.
- **Reasons, not decoration.** Every choice needs a reason (what a visitor feels, what it does for sign-up, what matches the rest). The owner will ask "why".
- **Real craft over shortcuts.** Cheap CSS gradients, glows and stickers read as fake to the owner. Use real rendering and measure things (sizes, contrast, light) instead of guessing.
- **One family.** The solar system, FREE!, the button and the form must look like they belong together. Today they do not.
- **Always give the downsides** with the upsides. Do not filter what you think they want to hear.

- Grey as the main colour (grey is not silver); "dirty" beige pearl; flat poster looks; satin or fabric folds as a background; grain; generic blue/purple/pink "esoteric" looks; round, pill, oval or rectangular buttons; colour blocks behind text; huge headlines; many near-identical options.
