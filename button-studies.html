Button studies

Six takes. All of them: the top is dished in, not domed out. The word is cut into the surface. Red appears only in the side walls, where the metal faces the page and reflects it. One horizon, no stripes. Every highlight is computed from the light above — move the light and all of them change together.

01 — Softened bar
Rounded rectangle, broad polished rim, deep dish. The most restrained of the six.
02 — Shallow cut
The end cuts, but shallow — so the ends are still face, not solid bevel. This is the current shape, fixed.
03 — Lozenge
Fully round ends. The rim never breaks, so the reflection runs continuously around it.
04 — Keyed
One end squared, one end cut. Asymmetric, so it reads as a specific object rather than a generic shape.
05 — Sunk plate
The button is a plate set down inside a raised collar, the way a machined control key sits in its panel. Two levels of relief instead of one.
06 — Tapered
Sides draft inward very slightly, like something cast rather than cut. The taper catches more red on one side than the other.
